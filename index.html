What is Bitcoin? How Does it Work? Pros & Cons

Bitcoin: The Digital Currency

Bitcoin is one of the most well-known examples of cryptocurrency and has had a major influence on the development of digital finance. Unlike traditional currencies such as pounds, euros and dollars, Bitcoin exists entirely in digital form and is designed to operate without a central bank controlling the network. Instead, it uses a combination of computer networks, cryptography and blockchain technology to record and verify transactions. Bitcoin was introduced in 2009 under the name Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym whose real identity remains unknown. Since its creation, Bitcoin has attracted interest from computer scientists, businesses, investors and members of the general public because of its different approach to transferring and storing value. It has also influenced the development of thousands of other cryptocurrencies and blockchain-based technologies. Understanding Bitcoin is therefore useful not only for learning about digital currencies, but also for understanding important computer science concepts such as distributed networks, cryptography, databases and digital security.

What is it?

Bitcoin is a decentralised digital currency that allows people to send and receive value over the internet. Instead of transactions being controlled and recorded by one central organisation, Bitcoin uses a distributed network of computers to maintain a shared record called the blockchain. When a Bitcoin transaction takes place, information about it is checked by the network and eventually recorded in a block. These blocks are connected together to form a chronological chain, making it possible to track the history of transactions on the network. Bitcoin uses cryptography to help secure transactions and control access to funds. People can interact with the Bitcoin network using digital wallets, which contain the information needed to manage their Bitcoin. A key feature of Bitcoin is its decentralised design, meaning that no single company or government operates the entire network. The system instead relies on rules implemented by its software and computers around the world that participate in maintaining the network.

Quick Facts

  • 📅 Created in 2009

    Bitcoin was introduced in 2009 by a person or group using the name Satoshi Nakamoto. The original Bitcoin software and a document explaining how the system worked were released in 2009. Nakamoto's real identity has never been conclusively established.

  • 🔢 Maximum Supply of 21 Million

    The Bitcoin protocol is designed so that no more than 21 million bitcoins will be created. This limit is different from currencies such as pounds or dollars, where the supply can be changed by central banks and governments. Individual bitcoins can also be divided into much smaller units called satoshis.

  • ⛓️ Uses Blockchain Technology

    Bitcoin transactions are recorded on a blockchain, which is a continuously growing digital ledger. Transactions are grouped into blocks, and each block is connected to the previous one. Copies of this record are maintained across computers participating in the Bitcoin network.

  • 🌐 Decentralised Network

    Bitcoin does not rely on one central bank or company to operate its network. Instead, computers around the world participate in maintaining and verifying the system according to Bitcoin's software rules. This distributed structure is one of the main differences between Bitcoin and traditional centralised payment systems.

  • 🔐 Uses Cryptography

    Cryptography is an important part of Bitcoin's security. It helps authenticate transactions and allows users to prove that they have control over the Bitcoin associated with their wallet. Public and private keys are used as part of this system.

  • ⛏️ Uses Proof-of-Work

    Bitcoin uses a process called proof-of-work to help secure its network and add new blocks to the blockchain. Participants known as miners use computing power to solve computational problems as part of the process. Successful miners can receive rewards according to the rules of the Bitcoin protocol.

  • 💻 Exists Digitally

    Unlike physical cash, Bitcoin does not exist as coins or notes that can be held in your hand. Ownership is represented through information recorded on the Bitcoin network, and users interact with their Bitcoin through digital wallets and cryptographic keys.

  • ₿ Can Be Divided Into Smaller Units

    A single bitcoin does not have to be transferred as a whole. Bitcoin can be divided into 100 million smaller units called satoshis. This allows very small amounts of Bitcoin to be represented and transferred.
